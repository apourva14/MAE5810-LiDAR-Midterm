LiDAR navigation validation

A physical dependency should first change a measurement, then alter navigation only when that information matters to the task. These checks separate those two claims.

1. Coverage and visibility are separate

The blue boundaries show the configured nominal angular field of view. A target-directed ray can remain inside that coverage and still be blocked: the nearest opaque surface terminates its line of sight. Dashed red continuations identify where the ray would reach the target if the foreground panel were absent.

2. Check detection where it is uncertain

At three recorded navigation poses, a real eligible target ray is replayed with independent receiver noise. Extinction is solved to place its mean received SNR at 11 settings from 6 to 14. With a threshold of 10, Gaussian receiver noise predicts PD = Φ(mean SNR − 10). Each setting has 1,000 trials.

Numerical check: probability RMSE = 0.00547; maximum absolute probability error = 0.01646 (1.65 percentage points). The model lies inside 32 of 33 pointwise intervals. Across 16,507 detected echoes, conditional range RMS is 32.36 mm, versus 32.29 mm predicted by the model.

This is conditional detection of an already visible, range-eligible target echo. It does not test whether geometry, occlusion, or angular sampling produces such an echo in the first place.

3. Test whether measurement losses matter to navigation

Each of three layouts uses its own fixed incomplete-map reference route. The controller, robot and task settings stay fixed while optical extinction or one sensor setting changes. Twenty paired receiver-noise seeds are run for every condition. Failures remain in all-run safety statistics. The largest extinction coefficients are deliberately severe optical stress tests and are not calibrated fog or rain conditions.

What these tests establish

The geometry diagram explains the FOV/LOS mechanism; near-threshold trials test calibration against the assumed stochastic model; the multi-layout navigation study tests task consequences under a frozen controller. Agreement establishes internal consistency and simulated task robustness under these conditions.

The sensor coefficients and weather model remain illustrative. Exact poses, static obstacles, snapshot scans and three hand-designed layouts limit external generalization. Noise seeds repeat stochastic measurements; they are not independent new environments. A 20/20 result is not proof of perfect reliability.
